## What each one actually does
- **Hormozi, Foundation (9.5M):** a physical prop metaphor carries the whole video. He builds a cardboard brick tower live, it falls, he rebuilds. Adds a circular countdown timer graphic, a black-and-white flashback for the past, a 3D skyscraper icon pop, and a fire explosion on the last beat. White caps captions, 3 words, centre. See [[Key Visual]], [[Grade Order]].
- **Hormozi, Always Be Ready (8.7M):** one locked shot, story told seated. Interest comes from punch-ins plus cut-out stickers that pop in on the noun (a boy in a suit, a white sports car). Yellow box captions with black text on his chest. See [[Callouts and Arrows]], [[Punch-in and Snapback]].
- **Dhruv Rathee (23M):** split screen with a cream panel on top and him on a warm orange backdrop below. Price stacking: the iPhone's price is shown as a growing pile of cut-out objects with rupee labels (ACs, TVs, fridges, buffaloes). No captions at all. See [[Unit Chart]], [[Split and Half Screen]].
- **Caleb Ralston (63K):** deliberately minimal. One podcast shot, small italic serif captions low in frame, a small rounded picture-in-picture card for references. Proves the restraint end of the spectrum. See [[Over-editing]].
- **Kallaway:** split screen by default, one giant white word on full-screen face beats, glow-rim cards for screen recordings. See the separate Kallaway note.

## Patterns across the batch
1. **Object metaphors beat graphics.** Both the 9.5M Hormozi and the 23M Dhruv turn the idea into physical or cut-out objects the viewer can count or watch fall.
2. **Hook density:** the top performers change the picture 6-9 times in the first 10 s, then relax.
3. **Captions are optional when the visual explains.** Dhruv's 23M short has none, because the objects carry the meaning.
4. **One locked shot can work** when stickers and punch-ins do the switching (Hormozi, Always Be Ready).

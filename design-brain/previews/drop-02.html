01 · X · @emilwidlund, 27 Oct

The storyboard is a code file

A designer makes 90-second launch films with no After Effects at all. One TypeScript file lists every scene as data. GSAP animates plain page elements with the brand's own easing. A headless browser steps through it frame by frame and pipes the frames to ffmpeg at 60 fps.

He directs Claude Code with taste notes like "this feels like someone clicking a button" or "no whooshes". A 1:30 film took about two hours and 60 prompts, and renders in six minutes.

02 · X · @theoardley on "You Built It", 1 Nov

Cut the world open like a cake

A brand-new TikTok page's first video got 13.1M views. Its 48 TikToks have 58.6M views since 11 October. The format is a block of ground sliced open like a cake, with a house on top. A calm, fast narrator builds an everyday thing one problem at a time, with one caption word on screen at a time.

It's the object-metaphor pattern taken to a cross-section. The viewer stays to see inside.

For you: cut open a local shop. Leads come in at the top, leak out through the cracks, and the reel shows where the money drains. That's the Atomz pitch as a picture.

03 · Reddit · r/MotionDesign, 1 Nov · 403 points in 4 days

Type that stays readable while it turns

Word flowers: every petal is a line of text and the flower spins in 3D. Within two hours a commenter rebuilt it and shared the rules that keep every word legible:

  • OWN THE RUNEach petal owns a fixed run of words. As it turns edge-on it drops words from its tip rather than reshuffling.
  • FLIP LEFTText pointing left is set from the other end, so nothing ever reads backwards.
  • HIDE WHOLEA word behind another petal hides completely, never half-cut.
For you: any 3D or rotating title. Never paint words onto a turning surface as a texture.
Source: FrameCall write-up · links into Physics Type, Decode Type, Caption System
04 · Reddit · r/VideoEditing + Sycamore retention study, Oct

Pause grammar

Editors agree on one line: "a pause before a line is doing work, a pause after one is usually dead." The rules that follow from it:

  • 0.15–0.25 sGap between two sentences. Anything longer goes.
  • 0.3–0.6 sKeep this pause right before a number, reveal or joke.
  • 115–120%Punch in on every second or third cut, never every cut.
  • J-CUTStart the next line's audio a few frames early, so a removed pause isn't also a visual jump.
  • 180–200 wpmA tight talking head. At 130 wpm, a third of the clip is silence.

Jenny Hoyos cut one second from a Short and retention went from about 83% to 88%.

For you: your 81% skip comes from the first 3 seconds. Start on your first word, keep the pause only before the punch, and punch in on line two.

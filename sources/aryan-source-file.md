## 1. What he wants

- Build a personal brand from **0 followers and 0 following**, using Instagram or YouTube, and monetise it.
- **Main goal: become an excellent storyteller.** "I want to master the art of storytelling now."
- He loves how **Kallaway, Caleb Ralston and Sam Gaudet** tell stories. Their storytelling skills are "top notch". He also asked for **Aayush Swamy** to be analysed.
- Before making content, he wants to understand how storytelling works: "what I should know in the world of storytelling, which enables me to tell better stories and capture the viewers."
- He wants to connect with people way ahead of him in the software and AI industry.
- He wants the audience to become a **ready-made customer base** that buys. It can be used as leverage to start something, to connect with his current business, or for future businesses. His example: Iman Gadzhi, where a handful of followers buy whenever he launches.
- He took this idea from an influencer's Reel: first be clear on what your personal brand will create.
- Topics he wants to be in: **AI, automation, software, and building a personal brand around himself**. "Right now the wave is AI automations. The person who knows AI will dwarf other people who don't know how to use AI."

- Why: "I want to be free. I didn't want to work for some other person. I want to work for me and build my own legacy." The word **"legacy"** is important to him.
- He wants to be his own boss, be more productive, "reach many of the grey areas and fix them," and talk with people way ahead of him to learn from them.
- **First story he'd tell:** the change in mindset. "The first battle that I won was up in my mind." When he says he started a company, people say "Oh" and think it's hard. "It is hard, but if your mind is firm, you can very well achieve things."

## 10. Stories (his words, lightly cleaned)

### 10.1 The cold-calling saga
1. **Interior designers.** "I was cold calling interior designers and I was well into the calling. I had completed maybe 300 to 350 calls in that industry only. Everybody was telling one word to me on the call, which was no. And I felt: is this the right industry to pitch in?"
2. **Asking for direction.** "I talked with some people: called 2–3 friends who have factories, and one person I believe is my mentor, because he's been in the sales industry for the past 25 years. I asked them: is this the right direction I'm going in?" Then he researched with Claude whether manufacturers were the right next move. The answer was "yes, you can give it a shot."
3. **Manufacturers.** "I switched industry and started calling manufacturers. When the count reached 100, I stopped, because I knew that if I'm doing that much volume, I should get at least 10 people who are interested, which was not there."
4. **Going offline.** "Some of my friends who already have a company told me that cold calling is not working in India, and there's very little luck surface area in cold calling. So I started going offline, store to store, in my locality, Hiranandani."

### 10.2 The first store
- "The moment I walked into the first store, I had that nervousness before the first meeting, and I didn't know what I was going to say, because earlier I had made a script."
- "I had only one thing in my mind: your mind is designed to take the path of least resistance, so you have to do the opposite."
- "I just went in and pitched, got over my script, and it went pretty well."
- "The first 10 conversations were just understanding things. Then I started pitching."

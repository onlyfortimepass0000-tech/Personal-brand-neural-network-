# Jun Yuh — research notes

Collected 2026-11-02 for the Story Neural Network. His numbers are his own public claims. Where a framework's internals were not stated publicly, that is marked.

## Who he is

- Jun Yuh. Korean-American, grew up in what he describes as a "broken and poor immigrant family" with lifelong debt. Studied biomedical engineering at Drexel (BS + MS, magna cum laude) and walked away from the pharma path.
- ~8M followers across Instagram, TikTok and YouTube (@jun_yuh ~5.2M on Instagram). IG name line: "Jun Yuh | Personal Branding & Content Strategy".
- Founder of **Creator College** (with Shan Hanif), reported at $11-13M in revenue inside about 15 months. Ran **Creator Live London**, a sold-out 1,300-person event. Partners include Red Bull, Meta, ManyChat. Wrote a #1 Amazon new-release book.
- Grew his first ~3M followers on a shattered iPhone 12. Paid off his student loans and gave his father a $500,000 cheque — a story he tells on camera.
- Started with fashion and fitness content that went nowhere. The breakthrough was telling his own struggles. His biggest videos (36M, 25M, 24M views) are all variations of the same "becoming" story.

## Style and voice

- Tone is warm, sincere, teacherly. Opens live sessions with "Hello, beautiful people! Are we ready to rock?"
- Talks about faith openly ("embracing the cross") and frames content as service.
- Third-party analysis of his reels: average ~46s, moderate pace with clear beat progression, authoritative and practical tone, static medium close-ups for talking head, alternating with handheld lifestyle B-roll, jump cuts to keep long monologues alive, fast cuts synced to music, text with borders for readability, anticipatory music that signals the transformation.
- Hook archetypes seen across his reels: contrarian declarations challenging industry norms; vulnerable confessions of sudden crisis; visual misdirection that subverts expectations.
- Captions are lowercase, line-broken, teaching-first, and almost always end in a follow or a comment-word CTA ("comment STORY", "comment ADLEY").

## The storytelling frameworks (his core)

### L.I.F.E. — where stories come from
Every personal story fits at least one of four buckets: **Love, Identity, Fitness, Earnings.** Don't know what to post? Pick a letter first, then pick a story. He says it is even better when two buckets overlap.

### The three whys
After picking the story, ask **"why" three times**. The first answer is surface. The second is more real. The third is where you are vulnerable.

### The Storytelling Wheel — safe → real → raw
- **Safe**: a surface-level description of the events that happened.
- **Real**: more honest, partway down.
- **Raw**: the *why* and the deeper emotions around the story.
- His line: the safe story "will probably get you attention, while the other will get people to relate to you at a deeper level."
- His grading: bad creators stay on the surface; average creators jump around; the best stay in the raw.

### The 3P story arc — Problem, Pursuit, Payoff
His stated best arc. His own caption: "most creators make the mistake of starting their videos with personal successes to earn the viewer's trust, however it's actually turning viewers away." His example of the mistake: opening with the fancy car and the house. Lead with the problem, because shared struggle is what makes people care. The 3Ps are then packaged into repeatable formats.

### Silent Film storytelling format
No talking. On-screen text, B-roll and music only.
- Start with pain, because humans universally connect with struggle — it creates instant empathy.
- You do not need perfect footage from the actual moment; describe the emotional pain in compelling text.
- Skip trending sounds. Pick audio with **rising action** that builds (he likes Adele's "Skyfall").
- **The music drop is the payoff moment.** Pain → pursuit → payoff.
- Anchored in real personal story, never tips or hacks.

### Two hard lines on personal storytelling
1. **No one else is a prop.** None of his storytelling videos speak badly about his mother, his father or an ex. Every story stays on his own emotional, spiritual and physical maturation. He keeps his girlfriend's name private. Nobody can accuse him of monetising someone else's story because he never tells someone else's story.
2. **Always land on the transformation, never park on the pain.** The story must resolve into "here's who I became because of it." That is what stops it being sappy and makes it useful to the viewer.

## Positioning and system frameworks

### Creator Vision — one page, three branches
- **What** — your message and your content pillars. His message: "Your personal brand is your greatest asset." One message can legitimately support five pillars.
- **Who** — your avatar, demographic and psychographic.
- **Why** — your own stories. He calls a blank Why branch the entire gap between 100K followers and a million; "what do I post today?" is a symptom of it. The bottom of the sheet becomes a permanent bank of content starters.
- Deliberately low-tech: a ~30-minute exercise on paper.

### Become the niche ("Niche is dead, narrative wins")
Traditional advice says pick a micro-topic and stick to it. His objection: you evolve, your interests shift, your story expands, and the niche boxes you in. Instead **become the niche** — build the brand on your story, values and message, and create around your pillars, so the audience grows with you.

### 3-1-2-1 — seven posts a week, each with a job
- **Attract ×3** — storytelling, relatable or emotionally compelling, usually pain or struggle. Brings new viewers.
- **Nurture ×1** — real life in real time, behind the scenes, doubts, unpolished. No lesson, no transformation arc. He names this the release valve that stops storytelling calcifying into a highlight reel of curated wounds.
- **Position ×2** — insights, systems, lessons. Authority.
- **Convert ×1** — a clear path to buy, join or work with him.
- His line: "If you're posting seven times a week, and you follow this 3-1-2-1 structure, you'll build both trust and traffic."

### Expert POV vs Journey POV
Either share proven systems from success (**Expert**), or share lessons mid-journey, transparently (**Journey**) — e.g. "I've posted for six months and lost 100 followers, here's what NOT to do."

### The Younger You principle
When you don't know what to make, create for the version of yourself from two to three years ago.

### Study format, not substance
Learn from creators in any industry by studying the **format**, not their story. His checklist: hooks, visuals and editing, cadence, audio, story arc, CTAs. Then plan content as dates × ideas from your Creator Vision × studied formats.

### Production system
- **Three batch phases:** pre-production (scripting and ideas), production (filming in batches with reusable B-roll), post-production (editing, repurposing, uploading).
- **Scene Library:** a shared Dropbox with 50+ labelled folders ("Jun writing", "Jun walking", "Young Jun working") so the team assembles videos without filming daily.
- **"Complexity kills consistency."** Equipment is an excuse; the repeatable system is the asset.

### Valuable, not viral
Separate attention content from trust content. Build products from what the audience actually asks for — he surveyed his audience twice a week for three months before launching. Older content keeps selling, so every post needs a purpose (evergreen positioning).

### Monetisation = Alignment × Audience
Free lead magnet → ~$99 digital product → mid-ticket cohort coaching → high-ticket consulting and done-for-you.

### Jun's Four Wins
One mental, one physical, one spiritual and one accountability win per day.

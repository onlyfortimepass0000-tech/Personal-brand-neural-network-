## How each table reads

Top block (the stage): headcount, your role, leadership structure, number of companies at this stage.
Middle block: eight functions, each with a CONSTRAINT (what is broken) and TO GRADUATE (what fixes it).
Last row: one "Bottom line" sentence for the constraint and one for the graduation move.

The eight functions: Product, Marketing, Sales, Customer Service, Information Tech (IT), Recruiting, Human Resources (HR), Finance.

## Stage by stage, all eight functions

### 0: Improvise (researcher, just you)
- Product. Constraint: you have nothing to sell. Graduate: make something FREE to give away.
- Marketing. No one knows about your stuff. Tell people you have FREE stuff.
- Sales. You do not know how to sell. Get people to try it for free.
- Customer service. No customers to service. Service your free customers.
- IT. You have no tech. Get a computer, internet, microphone, phone, email.
- Recruiting. You do not know how to work. You recruit yourself to work.
- HR. You are unprotected. Create an entity to separate your personal assets from your business assets.
- Finance. Your personal money is the business's money. Get a business bank account, put money in, write off start-up expenses to save on taxes.

### 1: Monetize (starter, still just you)
- Product. Not good enough to sell. Fix it until it is good enough to sell. Make a V1 product.
- Marketing. No one knows you have PAID stuff for sale. Tell people you have paid stuff. Sign up for free ad spend credits.
- Sales. You still do not know how to sell. Use good results from free people to sell paid people. Offer to solve their problems for money.
- Customer service. Free customers do not like it or use it. Figure out all hidden costs (why they do not like or use it) by talking to them.
- IT. No basic software, tools or social media. Figure out the (free) software you need by joining free groups and reading online forums.
- Recruiting. You do not know how to do something or have the time to do it. Reach out to people you know to help, or use freelancer platforms.
- HR. You pay freelancers to get work done and do not get what you want. Create basic vendor agreements and expectations.
- Finance. You have no way to collect money. Set up a payment processor, run a payment, get a deposit.

### 2: Advertise (doer, freelancers)
- Product. Paying customers have higher expectations; product is unreliable and not good enough. Fix it good enough for now in an unscalable way.
- Marketing. Lead flow is inconsistent (stop:start). Live by the Rule of 100. Prioritize advertising daily.
- Sales. Sales conversions are inconsistent. Nail down a script for setting and closing: CLOSER and ACA.
- Customer service. Paid customers have higher standards and complain more. Learn to deal with disappointed customers (Angry boat). Get testimonials from happy customers (Epiphany script).
- IT. No money to afford tech. Use free tech (Google Suite), get free trials and credits for starter software. Website and hosting. Starter CRM/POS.
- Recruiting. Too much work for part-timers. Convert part-timers to full-timers, make public posts, or run ads on job boards to get full-timers.
- HR. You have full-time employees but structured like contractors. Set up W-2s and 1099s.
- Finance. You do not keep track of money or have a way to pay taxes. Spin up a QuickBooks equivalent. Save for taxes. Check your bank account daily.

### 3: Stabilize (trainer, first full-time hires)
- Product. No time to fix everything that is wrong. Fix the thing they complain about most.
- Marketing. Leads are not buying fast enough (colder). Add long-term nurture (emails and/or content).
- Sales. Wasting time with bad leads; not enough time to talk to good leads. Decide on basic qualifications. Outsource setting and calendaring.
- Customer service. New customers feel lost. Learn to onboard customers properly.
- IT. A bunch of different tools you do not use. Figure out the useful ones, extend free trial credits and discounts, cut the rest. Basic sales pipeline.
- Recruiting. Not getting quality applicants. Learn to write a job description and review resumes.
- HR. You are not withholding employee taxes and fees. Get a payroll provider so all taxes and fees by state are handled.
- Finance. People getting paid informally. Set up payroll processing, basic bookkeeping, managing invoices and payments.

### 4: Prioritize (manager, 1 layer: first team)
- Product. You said yes to anyone who would pay; feedback from so many different customers creates too many product directions. Specialize the product (V2) and price to serve a niched-down avatar.
- Marketing. Too many unqualified leads. Make better free stuff and be more creative to boost volume. Add qualification and friction so the extra volume self-selects.
- Sales. Speed to contact drops; no metrics around sales. Track basic KPIs manually on a spreadsheet: contact rate, scheduled, show, offer, close, cash collected.
- Customer service. No metrics for customer success. Install tracking and KPIs. Record and centralize notes for each customer in a CRM.
- IT. New team is not using everything. Get everyone on the platforms, protect passwords, get a project-management tool and a work chat tool.
- Recruiting. Wasting time with bad candidates. Learn to run a proper interview and check references.
- HR. Employees do not know how to behave (PTO, sick days, dress code, code of conduct). Employee handbook and policies.
- Finance. Cash flow is lumpy from random one-time expenses. You find out businesses cost money to run. Set up P&L and cash flow statements. Get basic business insurance.

### 5: Productize (director, 1.5 layers: first 2-3 managers)
- Product. Customers have nothing else to buy from you and churn. Connect customer service and product. Make something new to sell them.
- Marketing. Qualified leads are too expensive and cap your ability to advertise. Spend more on niched-down messaging with a new offer and raise the CAC limit. Start scoring your leads (you select). Attribution plus CRO.
- Sales. Customers get sold with unrealistic expectations; refunds and bad reviews become an issue. Create sales aids and materials. Install a weekly game tape between CS and Sales/Marketing for QA and feedback. Track NPS, CSAT and refund rate by rep.
- Customer service. Customers come in with unrealistic expectations; inconsistent answers. Build a proper CS playbook to onboard and train the team to answer questions the same way. Weekly game tape review. Customer ascension path.
- IT. Employees use personal hardware and software, which creates issues when people leave. No tracking for leads and customers interacting with the business. Company-owned and controlled hardware and software. Need a dashboard and reporting system.
- Recruiting. Trouble finding higher-level roles and managers. Deliberate promotions from within rather than Peter Principle. Incentivize employee referrals (CAT = cost to acquire talent).
- HR. You are firing people incorrectly and are exposed. Create termination policies and process.
- Finance. You do not know how much money you can reinvest into growth. Make a budget (save for big expenses), simple financial forecasting, begin tracking tax write-offs.

### 6: Optimize (leader, 2 layers: full team of managers)
- Product. Improving two products slows down improvements. Install an incremental product-improvement process for both products.
- Marketing. Ads do not convert as well to colder traffic; CAC goes up. Implement an ad assembly process to increase volume.
- Sales. Closing efficiency on the core product drops and becomes inconsistent. Refunds go down but so do closes. Install a sales training system, individual coaching and team cadence.
- Customer service. Churn increases from volume and the new product. Create customer segmentation by cohort and activation points.
- IT. New people coming in, old people leaving with IP, data and access. Cyber security, malware, password protection from and for employees. Onboarding and offboarding.
- Recruiting. The number and type of roles you need exceed your current network (everyone who is good already has a job). Learn to headhunt higher-level roles. Add multiple interviews filtering for different skills.
- HR. You cannot get higher-level talent because they expect a full compensation package. Set up benefits, retirement and advanced compensation plans.
- Finance. You are spending money to grow, but you are not growing. Money is missing; where is my return? Scale up AR/AP. Get more granular financial data. Balance sheet. Begin quarterly tax payments.

### 7: Categorize (executive, 3 layers: leadership team)
- Product. Product outdated; improvements take too long to roll out. Fix one problem at a time with focused, feature-specific teams.
- Marketing. Lead flow is volatile because you only have one way to get customers. Fund a second acquisition channel with a referral process (and track it) and segmented long-term nurture by lead score. Pick the most similar platform, or a new core 4.
- Sales. Leads getting wasted; not worked consistently. Better schedule coverage. Create a lead-nurture checklist and kudos for top show rates.
- Customer service. CS is only reactive; all firefighting. Create a proactive customer journey and communication. Focus on renewals and ascensions.
- IT. Data, IP and access all over the place and disorganized. Network technology. Bring solutions to the cloud. Data host. Centrally locate hardware if on site.
- Recruiting. Takes too long to hire good people and they get hired elsewhere; too many open roles to keep track of. Install an Applicant Tracking System. Pay specialized recruiting firms to keep up.
- HR. Too many different places for employee information and you have no data. Set up an HRIS (Human Resource Information System).
- Finance. Your team starts spending your money stupidly. Quarterly expense management. Budgets by department. Cards with limits. Expense approval process. Add insurance coverage and get fancier projections.

### 8: Specialize (president, 4 layers: executive team)
- Product. Too many features overwhelm customers. Survey, prune, improve through deletion. Pruned items can become cross-sells.
- Marketing. Broader creative brings in lower-quality leads and dilutes the brand; CAC goes up again. Grow the brand with standardized messaging, free value and big positive brand associations.
- Sales. Closing efficiency on the premium product drops. Route best leads to best closers (front end and ascensions). Automated metrics tracking.
- Customer service. Multiple customer journeys conflict or are poorly timed with one another. Create proactive recommendations to cross-sell other products and a blended customer journey.
- IT. Departments outgrow general software and need specific solutions; too many internal inquiries; no inquiry process; departmental policies. Get a specialized stack for departments. Internal inquiry process. Staff up if needed. Departmental policies and service level agreements.
- Recruiting. Recruiters hold inconsistent standards and culture suffers; wrong candidates get too far in the process. Standardized selection process for all incoming talent. Founder final check.
- HR. So many new and existing employees that performance suffers; compliance issues and complaints skyrocket. Group onboarding (software). Documented performance management systems. Insurance (EPLI). Reinforce cultural values.
- Finance. You are still paying retail for everything and getting overcharged. Cash is unprotected and getting 0% returns. Re-negotiate rates on everything. Cash to yield accounts. Specialize finance roles (especially tax mitigation). Internal financial audits cadence.

### 9: Capitalize (chairman, 5 layers: CEOs)
- Product. Changes to successful products (cash cows) make them worse, but you need new products to keep growing. Buy (M&A) or build (R&D) the next rising-star product to add to the suite (1+ year out).
- Marketing. Lead attribution by channel becomes difficult; platform and regulatory compliance become an issue. Shift to brand-first advertising with an omnichannel approach and blended ROAS across all products. Install a compliance process.
- Sales. Too many different leads and products for any one type of salesperson. Salespeople specialize in selling different products to different leads using different processes. Route leads by product or territory.
- Customer service. Support for varied newer offerings is breaking from high volume. Triage support to specialized CS reps to improve efficiency and shorten training.
- IT. Old tech stack insufficient and needs update; departments demand new and specialized solutions. Hire more specialized tech roles for each major tech function with an updated and segmented tech stack. You may need a new enterprise-level software.
- Recruiting. Best talent does not respond to standard recruiting efforts from anyone besides the CEO/founder. Founder directly attracts the highest-level roles and sells on growth and vision (6+ month cycle to feel comfortable).
- HR. Churn increases and you do not know who you need to grow. Strategic workforce planning: org, capacity planning, backfill, skill gaps, role redundancy, from generalists to hyper-specialization.
- Finance. Your finances are not legit enough to get other people to give you money (raise, borrow, buy) to make a big investment in M&A or R&D. "Prep for sale": get a QofE, banker audit, capital structure. Hedge big investments. Optional: raise cash for M&A or to fuel growth.

## Patterns across the ladder (mine)

1. One bottleneck per stage. The last row names a single problem and a single move. Everything else in the table is a consequence of that one move.
2. Every fix creates the next constraint. Free stuff leads to a first sale, which creates inconsistent leads, which creates overload, which forces help and then focus. Stage 3 hiring creates the management problem of stage 4.
3. The order of constraints repeats a cycle: get customers (0-2), get capacity (3), get focus (4), get more per customer (5), get efficient (6), get organised (7), get specialised (8), get capital for a bet (9).
4. Marketing ladder: free stuff, then paid stuff with free ad credits, then daily advertising (Rule of 100), then long-term nurture, then qualification friction, then niche messaging with a higher CAC limit and lead scoring, then an ad assembly process, then a second acquisition channel with referrals, then brand-first with standardised messaging, then omnichannel blended ROAS.
5. Sales ladder: try it free, then use free results to sell, then a script (CLOSER, ACA), then qualify and outsource setting, then KPIs on a spreadsheet, then sales aids and weekly game tape with NPS/CSAT/refund by rep, then training system and coaching, then lead-nurture checklist, then routing best leads to best closers, then specialising salespeople by product.
6. Customer service is the product's feedback loop at every stage: talk to free customers (1), handle angry customers and collect testimonials (2), onboard (3), KPIs and a CRM (4), playbook and ascension path (5), cohorts (6), proactive journey and renewals (7), cross-sell journeys (8), triage to specialists (9). The same weekly "game tape" meeting links CS to Sales/Marketing at stage 5.
7. Product ladder: free thing, V1, unscalable fix, fix the top complaint, V2 for a niche, second premium product, incremental improvement process, feature teams, prune, acquire or build the next star.
8. The finance and HR rows are compliance catch-up: each stage fixes something that was already wrong (personal money mixed with business money, contractors that are really employees, informal pay, no insurance, no budget). Most of it is plumbing, not growth.
9. Stage 4 is the only stage whose fix is to say no (niche down). Stage 8 is the other (prune features). Stage 5 and 9 are the "make something new" stages.
10. Stages 1 to 3 are the only ones that can be run on free tools and credits. The slides name the free option at each: free product, free ad credits, free groups and forums, Google Suite and free trials, vendor agreements, a payment processor.
11. The Project's `alex_hormozi_complete_guide.md` covers the Rule of 100 and CAC/LTV/payback. Terms named on these slides but not defined on them: CLOSER, ACA, Angry boat, Epiphany script, game tape, Core 4, CAT, CRO. Check them before quoting.

## Caveats (mine)

- The tables use US terms: W-2, 1099, state payroll tax, QuickBooks, EPLI, "30,000,000 companies". For India the same stages need local equivalents (entity type, GST, TDS, PF/ESI, a current account). Do not copy the compliance rows without checking with a CA.
- Company counts per stage read as "companies that reach this stage". The slides do not say where the numbers come from.
- "9 stages" in Aryan's caption is the numbering 1-9; the tables run 0-9.

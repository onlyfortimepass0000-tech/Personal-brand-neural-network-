## Adley Kinsman — the retention engineer

**Who:** Founder of Viralish, Nashville. 1.4M on Instagram, 2.75M on YouTube. Claims 1B+ organic views a month since 2020, for brands like Land Rover, Raising Cane's and Frito-Lay. Broke out of "3,000-view jail" with a chicken in a bathtub: 19M views overnight.

**How she speaks:** Fast, warm, self-deprecating, "I'm begging you", "you guys". Talks like a creator who failed a lot, not a guru. But every claim lands on a number — retention percentages, drop-off seconds, test counts. She breaks content down like a scientist wearing a party hat.

**How her reels are built:** A branded frame states the credential and the promise before she opens her mouth ("1B+ view/month creator teaches how to use props"). Short talking-head, usually holding a prop. Caption is the same every time: topic → "if you're new here, I'm Adley, founder of Viralish" → a proof number → free thing → "comment AUDIT".

**Her six steps:** (1) Hook that holds 90% for six seconds, built by the **combo method** — two things that don't go together. Every hook must trigger an emotion *and* open a gap. (2) **Missy Elliott method** — design the video backwards from the first three seconds; open on a scene, never on your conclusion. (3) **Payoff** in the final 3-10 seconds, not before. (4) **Engineer the comments** — decide what people should say, then plant the triggers. (5) **Raise the stakes** with four levers: location, props, wardrobe, casting. (6) **Split test** — 3-4 versions on trial reels, 24-72 hours, publish the winner.

**The number that matters:** a 40% retention drop names the exact beat to rewrite.

**What's new for you:** she is the only one of the six who treats a video as a testable unit rather than a one-shot. Trial reels are free, so this is a method, not a budget. Her stakes levers are also the cheapest things in the network — a doorway, a prop, what you wear.

## Jun Yuh — the story excavator

**Who:** Korean-American, biomedical engineering at Drexel, walked away from pharma. ~8M followers. Grew the first 3M on a shattered iPhone 12. Founder of Creator College, reported $11-13M in about 15 months. Paid off his loans and handed his father a $500K cheque — a story he tells on camera.

**How he speaks:** Sincere, teacherly, unhurried. Lowercase captions, line-broken, teaching-first. Average reel ~46s, static medium close-up, jump cuts, bordered text, music that rises into the turn. Opens big rooms with "Hello, beautiful people!"

**His frameworks — the part you asked for:**

| Framework | What it is |
|---|---|
| **L.I.F.E.** | Every personal story sits in one of four buckets: Love, Identity, Fitness, Earnings. Stuck? Pick a letter, then a story. Better when two overlap. |
| **Three whys** | Ask why three times. First answer is surface, second is honest, third is where you're actually vulnerable. |
| **Storytelling wheel** | Safe → real → **raw**. Safe gets attention; raw gets people to relate. Bad creators stay on the surface, average jump around, the best stay raw. |
| **3P arc** | **Problem, Pursuit, Payoff.** Open on the problem. |
| **Lead with the problem** | Opening with your success — the car, the house — pushes people away, even though it feels like credibility. |
| **Silent film format** | No talking. Text + B-roll + rising music. Start with pain. The music drop *is* the payoff. |
| **Two hard lines** | No one else is a prop (he never tells anyone else's story, keeps his girlfriend's name private). Always land on the transformation, never park on the pain. |
| **Creator Vision** | One page, three branches: What (message + pillars), Who (avatar), Why (your stories). A blank Why branch is the gap between 100K and a million. |
| **Become the niche** | Niching down boxes you in as you change. Build on story, values and message; the audience grows with you. |
| **3-1-2-1** | Seven posts a week with jobs: 3 attract, 1 nurture (real life, no lesson), 2 position, 1 convert. |
| **The younger you** | Make it for yourself from 2-3 years ago. |
| **Study format, not substance** | Steal structure from any industry: hook, visuals, cadence, audio, arc, CTA. Never steal the story. |
| **Complexity kills consistency** | Equipment is an excuse. Three batch phases + a scene library of 50+ labelled B-roll folders, so nobody has to film to post. |
| **Valuable, not viral** | Separate attention content from trust content. He surveyed his audience twice a week for three months before building a product. |

**What's new for you:** he's the closest analogue in the network — no gear, own origin story, business built off the audience. Three of the four L.I.F.E. buckets are already live in your file (Identity, Fitness, Earnings). And his "no one else is a prop" rule is the framework behind a decision you already made: family stays off camera.

## The two tensions worth testing

1. **Jun vs the other four.** The network already held "prove it before they doubt it" as a pattern all four original creators teach. Jun says leading with your success turns people away. Read closely, he objects to opening on *status* — the car, the house — not to proof that you understand the viewer's problem. Narrower than it first looks, and the first thing worth a mix test.
2. **Jun vs Adley on where a video starts.** Jun starts from the truth and finds the format. Adley starts from the opening three seconds and reverse-engineers the truth into it. Same destination, opposite doors.
